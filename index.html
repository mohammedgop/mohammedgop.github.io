01 / PROFILE

About me

I am an applied mathematician with a PhD from the Department of Mathematics and Statistics at Georgia State University. My research brings together dynamical systems, numerical simulation, and quantitative analysis to study biological and physical processes.

My doctoral work explored locomotor control across scales, linking motoneuron dynamics, biomechanical models, and turning behavior. My research also includes mathematical epidemiology, parameter estimation, identifiability, uncertainty quantification, and infectious disease forecasting.

02 / SCHOLARSHIP

Research interests

Using rigorous mathematics and computation to address questions in biology, movement, and health.

01

Computational neuroscience

Neuronal excitability, motoneuron dynamics, bistability, and multiscale mechanisms of movement control.

02

Biomechanics and locomotion

Mechanistic models of quadrupedal locomotion, turning behavior, and interactions between neural commands and physical movement.

03

Mathematical epidemiology

Dynamical models, parameter estimation, and computational analysis for infectious disease spread and public-health applications.
